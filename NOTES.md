# NOTER — Selahs danske gengivelse (selah-da)

Denne fil holder **de vers, hvor et menneskes hånd eller en afgørelse har rørt
gengivelsen** — så en læser, der undersøger et vers, kan se, om det er lavet af
maskinen, med hånden eller under en afgørelse, og hvorfor. Den holder også
afgørelserne om bestemte ord, de kendte fejl, der endnu står åbne, og de
spørgsmål, der venter på et dansk øre.

Hver post giver verset · hvad der blev gjort · hvorfor · datoen. Hebraisk står
i hver post. **Posterne er skrevet på engelsk**, fordi de citerer hebraisk
morfologi, og engelsk er arbejdssproget for den disciplin, der går på tværs af
alle sprogene. Poster rettes; de fjernes ikke i stilhed — arkivets historie er
en del af vidnesbyrdet.

Læs `PROVENANCE.md` for, hvordan teksten blev lavet, og `CONTRIBUTING.md`, før
du retter noget.

**Teksten bliver efterset netop nu.** Vers, hvis række ikke passede til det
hebraiske, er taget ud og bliver gengivet på ny. Listerne nedenfor er derfor
ikke endelige.

## §1 · Verses written by hand

Every other verse was rendered by the machine under the written rules.

- **Deuteronomy 5:13** · `ששת ימים תעבד ועשית כל מלאכתך` · written by hand ·
  2026-10-03 · the machine returned this verse **wholly in German three times**
  (*Sechs Tage sollst du arbeiten …*) — the fourth commandment — the third time
  correcting itself mid-sentence. The flow was written from the verse's own
  clean Danish rows: *Seks dage skal du arbejde og gøre alt dit arbejde.* The
  file's `tier` field does not say `hand`; this entry is the record.
  **Awaits a Danish ear.**

## §2 · Words fixed during the work — a named exchange, not a general rule

Each was fixed in the rules (`da.md`) by name, because a general rule ("no
`ä ö`") did not hold against a word the machine had learned in Swedish.

- **`ארון` · `ארן` (the ark of the covenant) → `arken`**, never `Aron` ·
  2026-10-03 · Exodus 25:14, 16 had rendered the ark as *Aron* —
  a transliteration of הארן that collides with Aharon's church name, so the
  reader sees Aharon. Those verses were rendered again; Exodus 25:14 now reads
  *… for at bære ⟨את⟩ arken med dem.*
- **`נשיא` · `אלוף` · `שר` → `høvding`** (with **ø**) · 2026-10-03 · every
  chief and captain had come out in the Swedish spelling *hövding*
  (Genesis 36:40, Numbers 7, Joshua 5:14–15, 1 Samuel 29:2–4). Genesis 36:40
  now reads *høvding Timna, høvding Alva, høvding Jetet*.
- **`עבד` (bound) → `træl`**, never Swedish `träl` · and **`og`**, never German
  `und` · 2026-10-03.

## §3 · Known faults — open

These verses stand here so no one reads them as settled. Observed in the
corpus 2026-10-06, while the re-check is under way.

### The Name

- **Ezekiel 14:11** · `נאם אדני יהוה` · the flow ends *— Adonaj **Jahvs**
  ord*: a clipped genitive. Danish is **Jahves**. The rows are right
  (`Adonaj`, `Jahve`). The same verse glosses `בית` as *Israels hus* and then
  `ישראל` as *Jisrael* — the name twice, and once in the church spelling.
- **Psalm 24:9** · `שאו שערים ראשיכם … ויבא מלך הכבוד` · the Hebrew has no
  `יהוה`, but the flow inserts *og HERREN…* — the refused title, in a seat
  where the Hebrew has no Name at all — and then corrects itself in the text
  (*— ret: Lad kongen af herligheden komme ind*). The rows are clean. The flow
  must be rendered again.

### Norwegian and spelling — Isaiah 53:5

`והוא מחלל מפשענו מדכא מעונתינו` — the flow reads *Og han blev
**gennemboet** for vore **overtredelser**, knust for **vor** misgerninger …*

- *overtredelser* is the Norwegian spelling; Danish writes **overtrædelser**
  (Ezekiel 14:11 has it right).
- *gennemboet* for `מחלל` looks like a broken *gennemboret*.
- *vor misgerninger* — the plural wants **vore**, as in *vore overtredelser*
  in the same line.

These were seen in one verse quoted for this file. **The class is probably
wider; a Danish reader will find more.**

### The marker

- **Jeremiah 27:7** · `ועבדו אתו … ואת בנו` · the flow reads *tjene ham ⟨את⟩
  og hans søn ⟨את⟩* — in the flow the marker stands after its object, while
  the rows set `ואת` before `בנו` (*hans søn*). Row and flow disagree. Open.

### The rows

- Verses whose row of glosses did not match the count of Hebrew words, or held
  an empty gloss, or English inside ⟨ ⟩, were taken out and are being rendered
  again now. When that is finished, any that remain will be listed here.

## §4 · Lawful — do not correct

- **`å`** · shared by Danish, Swedish and Norwegian; never a sign of bleed.
- **`gud`, `guder`, `herre`, `min herre`** in lowercase · the nations' gods,
  an image, a human lord. Lawful; the Hebrew token decides.
- **`Bileams ord`** for `נאם בלעם` · before a human name, `נאם` is a man's
  word, not the prophet's formula `siger Jahve`.
- **`mincha`** is not used where `מנחה` is a gift or tribute (Genesis 43:11).
- **`himlene`** · plural, as Hebrew `שמים` is. See §5.
- **`לבן`** in Genesis 29 is a man, Lavan — not the colour white.

## §5 · Open questions — the machine has not chosen

Each waits on a Danish ear. Until then the text holds the form in the left
column.

1. **`Jahve`** — or **`Jahveh`** / **`Jahwe`**?
2. **`ruach` · `chesed` · `Mashiach` · `mincha`** — *ch* for `ח` and `כ`; or
   *h*?
3. **The names** (*Avraham* · *Jitschak* · *Moshe* · *Jerushalajim*) — Hebrew
   sound against the names every Danish reader knows. The largest change a
   Danish reader will see.
4. **`Shabbat`** — *sh* is not Danish orthography; or **`Sjabbat`**?
5. **`himlene`** (plural, as Hebrew `שמים`) — or *himlen*? Genesis 1:1 reads
   *I begyndelsen skabte Elohim ⟨את⟩ himlene og ⟨את⟩ jorden.*
